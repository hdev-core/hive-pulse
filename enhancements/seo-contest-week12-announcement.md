### The GEO score is getting harder from week 12

**Your GEO score is going to drop next week. The bar moved up — your writing did not get worse.**

Here is the problem, in one number. Across every entry this contest has ever scored, **57% of them held a perfect GEO 100**. More than half the field, maxed out. A score most people max is not telling anyone anything, and it is the real reason five of you kept tying on 200/200 and needing a tiebreak to separate you.

So from **week 12** the GEO checks get harder. Same checks, same names, nothing new to learn — the top band on each one now asks for more:

- Your **opening** has to be tighter. 12 to 45 words, or an explicit TL;DR.
- **Question headings** — two of them for full marks, where one used to do.
- **Definitions** — three, where two used to do.
- **Numbers and sources** — five figures with a source, where three used to do.

And there is **one new check, for personal posts only: Concrete detail.**

A story an AI can quote names things. "My friend helped me" is not quotable. "Ahmed walked me through it on Discord for four hours" is a fact an engine can lift and attribute to you. Name the people, the places, the dates, the numbers. That is the whole check.

**What this means in practice.** On the same posts, perfect scores fall from 57% of entries to about 9%. The median lands near 86. **100 is still reachable** — a handful of posts in this contest's history still earn it under the new rubric — but it has to be earned.

**And it makes ties rare again.** In weeks 9 and 10, five entries tied at the top of each round. Under the new scoring that drops to one. The Reading Ease tiebreak stays exactly as published, but it goes back to being a rare fallback instead of the thing deciding your podium.
