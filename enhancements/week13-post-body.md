### Two GEO checks were measuring the wrong thing. Fixed from week 13.

We said last week that the GEO score was getting harder, and it did - these three posts were scored on the new rubric. They took 100 anyway, and on the rubric as published they earned it. But a perfect board made us go and check the rubric itself, and we found two checks that were not doing what their names say. Both are fixed in HivePulse 1.16.0, and both apply **from week 13** - this round, announced here before it opens.

**1. "Concrete detail" was counting ordinary words as names.** That check looks for capitalised words to find the people, places and dates in your story. But the first word of every sentence is capitalised too - so *The*, *But*, *Thus* and *First* were being scored as names. Across every entry this contest has ever scored, **about half of what the check counted was capitalised only because a sentence started there**. One post banked *But* fifteen times and *The* fifteen times as "concrete detail". It now ignores those.

It works by position, not by a list of words, and that matters for this contest specifically. We tried a word list first and threw it out: it would have left Spanish and Urdu entrants scoring full marks on prose that names nothing, while an English entrant writing about a friend called **Will** or **Les** would have had a real name thrown away. Position treats every language the same and never penalises you for who you wrote about.

**2. A language tag in your title could hide your question mark.** HivePulse decides what kind of post you wrote from the title, and a question post is one that ends in `?`. A title like *"How Was Our Crazy 1 Day Canal Adventure? [Eng/Urdu]"* ends in `]`, so the question mark was invisible and the post was scored as a personal story instead. Question posts and personal posts are scored on different checks, so the wrong set was being applied. Four posts in this contest's history went down that path. The tag is now ignored when we look for the question mark.

**What this means for your score.** If you write personal stories, your GEO may dip a few points - the check is now measuring what it always claimed to. If you put a language tag after a question in your title, you will now be scored as an informational post: stricter checks, but the right ones for that kind of writing. Across all 77 entries we have ever scored, perfect GEO scores go from 13% to 8%, and the median moves 86 to 83. The two halves of the rubric also even out: personal posts were handing out perfect scores at 16% against 9% for informational ones, and both now sit at 8%.
