### We found a bug in our own judge, and it had the podium wrong

This needs saying plainly, because it moved 50 HIVE between two people.

Our judge was **rounding the combined score before ranking**. @nabbas0786 scored SEO 99 and GEO 100, which is a combined 99.5 - and the rounding turned that into 100. That made it look like a tie with @nursejoe's genuine 100, the reading-ease tiebreak was applied to break a tie that did not exist, and @nabbas0786 won it on ease 59 against 65. A lower score took third place from a higher one.

The rule we publish every week is **"Rank = (SEO% + GEO)/2"**, and 99.5 is not a tie with 100. The rounding was never part of the rule - it was an accident in our code. The comparison now uses the exact score, and the displayed number stays rounded as before. **Third place is @nursejoe's.**

We checked every round this contest has ever run. The bug could only ever bite when two entries sat within half a point of each other, and that has happened exactly once before - in week 8, between two entries in fifth and sixth place, neither of which was paid. **No past payout was affected, and no past result changes.**

@nabbas0786, this cost you 50 HIVE that our code had already shown you. We are sorry. You still take the X prize, and one point of SEO was the entire difference between fourth and the podium this week.

### Four perfect scores, and one that shows the rubric working

Three of five entries scored a perfect 200/200, and @nabbas0786 was one point away on 199. @ekads came in at SEO 98 and **GEO 88**, and that is the rubric doing its job rather than anyone writing badly - a 489-word post has less room to carry the named detail, question structure and sourced figures the GEO checks look for. Length is not the goal, but the checks need somewhere to live.
