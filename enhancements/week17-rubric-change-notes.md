# Week 17 — what to say about the readability change

Measured on 17 Nov 2026 with the shipped 1.18.0 engine, after week 16 was judged,
published and archived on the old rubric. **None of this re-scores week 16.** The archive
`contest-results-week16.csv` is the record of what was paid and stays that way.

## The honest headline is NOT "everyone scores lower"

That was the easy thing to say and the measurements do not support it. Two different
numbers move by very different amounts, and conflating them will scare people for nothing.

**Reading Ease — the number on the panel — does drop.** On week 16's own five entries:

| entrant | ease, as paid | ease, new rubric | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| @ahmedabbaci | 60 | 57 | −3 |
| @nursejoe | 60 | 57 | −3 |
| @nabbas0786 | 60 | 57 | −3 |
| @itx.wardah | 60 | 57 | −3 |
| @fluer | 73 | 72 | −1 |

Mean **−2.6**. Across all 99 posts ever entered it is **−4.16**, up to −16.

**The SEO score barely moves.** The same five posts go 100 → 99, 100 → 99, 100 → 99,
96 → 95, 89 → 89. Readability is 8 of the SEO points, so a few points of ease costs one
point of score. **The week 16 podium would be identical.** Say this plainly — it is the
part that stops people panicking.

## The line that makes the case

@ahmedabbaci reported that his diabetes post showed **60** in the editor and **57** once
published, and asked which was right. The answer, now measurable: **57 was right the whole
time.** The panel was inflating it by counting the line breaks in his editor as sentence
endings — breaks that vanish the moment markdown renders. The new number is not a lower
score, it is the number his post always deserved. The panel and the published post now
agree.

## What the change actually removes

Lead with the loophole, not the loss:

- **Five lines of `---` — 15 characters, no words — moved an entry from 99.5 to a perfect
  100.** That is the exact margin that moved 100 HIVE in week 14.
- Hard-wrapping a post at 60 columns was worth up to **+17 ease** and pushed 8 of the 99
  corpus posts into the top band.
- Fenced code was measured as one enormous sentence, so the score depended on whether the
  comments inside it began with `#`, `//`, `>` or `*` — an 8-point swing on identical prose.

Every one of those now pays **0.00**.
