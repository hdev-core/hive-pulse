### Half a point decided this round, and readability was the half point

Look at that table again. **Every single entry scored GEO 100.** Every one. The GEO rubric did not separate anybody, so the entire result came down to readability — three posts landed on ease 60 and two landed on 58 and 57, which cost them one SEO point each and the podium.

The change we announced last week did exactly what we said it would, in its first live round. @fluer and @itx.wardah wrote posts that are a half point from perfect. Under the old rule, where a line break counted as the end of a sentence, all five of you would probably have tied at 100 and taken 60 HIVE each.

**So the advice is concrete this week: shorter sentences.** Reading ease is the one number still doing real work, and the cheapest to move.

### We are dropping the 30-day account rule

The published rule said a referred person needed an account at least 30 days old. **@vaneex's account is 9 days old**, so the rule as written would have blocked the exact behaviour the rule was invented to encourage — someone genuinely new to Hive being brought in by someone already here.

A rule that pays you for recruiting people who were already on Hive, while refusing the ones who just arrived, has it backwards. **The 30-day requirement is removed from week 18.** The referred person still has to be new to this contest, and their entry still has to qualify.

We are also accepting @fluer's claim even though it came a week late, because the rule has never been claimed before and nobody could reasonably have known how strict we would be. **From week 18 the declaration belongs in your entry comment for that round.**

## What we fixed, because @itx.wardah pushed

She reported that her focus keyword changed when she reloaded the editor, and that her score changed with it: **100 before the reload, 96 after**. She was right, and there were three separate bugs behind it. All ours.

**Your keyword was never saved.** The keyword lived only in the open page, so a reload threw it away and the analyzer quietly picked a new one. Your choice is remembered now.

**The keyword it picked depended on timing.** The analyzer takes candidates from your title but ranks them by how often they appear in your body, so running before your post finished loading meant ranking candidates against an empty page. One draft produced three different keywords depending on when it fired. The analyzer now waits for both.

**And it let "the" be a focus keyword, scoring it a perfect 35/35.** That last one is worth understanding, because it means a number some of you have seen was too high. "the" scores perfectly for a reason that has nothing to do with writing: the word sits in your title, your URL, your subheading, your first sentence and your description — because the word sits in everyone's. Nobody searches for "the". **The contest judge has always refused words like it, which is why the published result was lower than the panel. The panel was wrong, not the result.** The panel now tells you a stopword cannot be a focus keyword instead of showing you a score you cannot win with.

**We paid @itx.wardah 100 HIVE for the report.** To be exact: a bounty for finding the bugs, not a change to her week 16 placing, which stands. Re-scoring a paid round would be unfair to the four people judged alongside her — the mistake that cost @nursejoe third place in week 14 and we are not repeating it. We would rather pay openly for a good bug report than fix it quietly and say nothing.

### One thing which is not a bug

**The judge cannot see the keyword you chose.** Only your post reaches the blockchain — your editor settings do not. So the judge picks the best keyword derivable from your title, and will never pick a stopword for you. **Panel and result can therefore differ slightly even when everything works.**

### What this is, in case you are new here

**HivePulse is a free browser extension** that scores a Hive post for Google and for AI assistants before you publish it. No paywall, no token, nothing to buy.

**We run this contest for two reasons, both true.** Hive's writing is close to invisible to Google and to ChatGPT, and we want more of it optimised. And every round tells us where our own scoring is wrong. Week 17 told us three things: our analyzer was accepting a focus keyword the contest would never accept, our referral rule was built to exclude the newest writers, and our own tool for reading your comments missed both referral claims and reported there were none. We found all three, fixed all three, and are telling you about all three - because a scoring tool that hides its own mistakes is not worth installing.
