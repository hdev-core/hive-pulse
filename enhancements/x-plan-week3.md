## Why week 2's posts underperformed

They were **contest announcements**. Every one asked the reader for something — install this, enter this, tag this — and gave nothing to a person who has never heard of Hive. The fix is not better wording. It is **having something to say**.

**1/**
We scored 14 blog posts twice: once on classic SEO, once on whether an AI would actually quote them.

The two numbers disagreed far more than we expected.

One post scored 93 on SEO and 60 on quotability. 🧵

**2/**
The disagreement isn't noise. Across all 14 posts the correlation is r = 0.73 — SEO explains only about half of quotability.

You can do everything Google asks and still be invisible to ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews.

**3/**
Here's every post. Orange is the SEO score, green is quotability, sorted by the size of the gap.

Look at the top row: 93 vs 60. Same post.

**4/**
The reason is mechanical, not mystical.

Google ranks **pages**. Answer engines quote **sentences**.

A page can be beautifully structured and still contain not one sentence that survives being lifted out of it.

**5/**
This is the whole thing in one rewrite:

"This shows a 40% improvement." → useless alone. What shows? Improvement in what?

"Weekly digests improved retention 40%." → stands by itself.

Same fact. Only one gets quoted.

**6/**
Three habits produced every perfect score in our data:

1. Open with the answer — payoff in the first 8–60 words
2. Write sentences that survive being quoted alone
3. Name your subjects — repeat the noun, drop the "it"

**7/**
The encouraging part: 6 of 14 posts scored a **perfect 100** on quotability.

It isn't talent. It's a checklist. Most writers have simply never been told the checklist exists, because the tooling has all been pointed at Google.

**8/**
We built the scorer into a free browser extension for Hive writers, and we're paying 300 HIVE a week to people who use it well.

Details in the reply. But the three habits above work anywhere you publish. 👇
